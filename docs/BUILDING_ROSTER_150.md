# Lowcar — 150-building plan

## Direction

**150 buildings total: the three approved studies plus 147 more.** A colorful, believable city built from related architectural families. Several buildings can be ordinary apartment blocks or simple shops. The variety comes from their proportions, facade rhythm, entrances, rooflines, balconies, and uses.

The approved corner market, terrace apartments, and Redline garage establish the finish: clean pastel walls, chunky cream trim, recessed blue windows, broad painted reflections, modest edge facets, and sparse architectural detail. Keep that finish across the whole set.

### Locked visual vision — Pastel Jazz City

**Pastel Jazz City** is our creative shorthand for Lowcar's direction. The mood blends the user's New Orleans, Broadway, Disneyland, brass-band, and jazz references: warm, lively streets with a little theatrical glamour. It should feel as though a trumpet band could come around the corner at any moment.

- **Pastel facades and creamy white trim are the signature.** Coral, mint, butter yellow, powder blue, and occasional lilac walls; substantial white window surrounds, cornices, balcony edges, and floor bands tie them together.
- **Architecture has some showmanship.** Use striped awnings, generous canopies, shallow stepped roof crowns, faceted rounded corners, occasional arched storefronts, and slender balcony rails. Small marquees and warm brass-colored accents suit selected businesses.
- **Plenty of buildings are completely ordinary.** Plain apartment blocks, modest offices, quiet houses, garages, and warehouses form most of the city. Consistent colors, trim, and proportions give those everyday buildings the same identity. A different doorway or window arrangement can be enough variation.
- **More elaborate facades are occasional accents.** A cinema entrance, corner café balcony, or station canopy gives a street character without making every building a landmark.
- **The finish stays bright, clean, and low-poly.** Broad surfaces, chosen facets, blue painted glazing, warm daylight, and clear shadow shapes. Decorative detail stays chunky enough to read beside the existing car.
- **The red muscle car remains the main character.** Its held poses, exaggerated weight shifts, squash, stretch, and swishes bring the animation energy to this welcoming city.
- **Music mood:** lively brass, jazz-band rhythm, and playful chase energy when audio is developed.

Apply this direction to the existing roster. The 150-building list remains the plan; normal buildings and shared architectural families are explicitly welcome.

### What makes a separate building

Each entry gets at least one clear architectural distinction, often two. Shared window pieces, doors, awnings, roof equipment, and construction methods are welcome.

- Two ordinary offices: one has a low blue roof cap and paired windows; the other has a cream facade frame and a small roof room.
- Two corner shops: one has a diagonal entrance and striped awnings; the other has a square corner, a continuous canopy, and apartments above.
- Two warehouses: one is a long low shed with loading doors along its side; the other is a taller, shorter block with a raised central roof.

Color supports those differences. Most buildings should still be distinguishable if they share the same wall color. Every building does not need a dramatic silhouette or a special gimmick.

### Shared visual rules

- **Forms:** substantial walls, convincing floor heights, thick roof edges, readable entrances. Low-poly through simple shapes and chosen facets.
- **Colors:** cream, coral, mint, butter yellow, powder blue, soft terracotta; occasional muted lilac. Navy glazing, blue or teal roof accents, restrained green plants. The car keeps the strongest red.
- **Details:** one or two main features per building. A balcony stack, a generous canopy, a stair tower, or a roof terrace can carry the identity.
- **Windows:** group them in deliberate rhythms. Mix tall pairs, broad storefront panes, recessed horizontal bands, and small service windows.
- **Roofs:** flat parapets, simple pitched roofs, setbacks, sawtooth roofs, and a few larger caps. Rooftops should look good when the car jumps above the street.
- **Surfaces:** clean matte color blocks. Sparse seams and large panels; avoid tiny grunge, busy texture noise, heavy outlines, and neon facade lighting.
- **Street scale:** keep the neighborhood low and cohesive. Most buildings are one to four stories, with broad, believable proportions. The skinny skyscraper example was permission for modest variation, not a direction to build tall towers. The existing five-story apartment remains a modest taller accent.
- **Scope:** exterior models with useful recesses and basic visible openings. Full interiors are separate work; an entire furniture department does not need to lurk behind a shop window.

## How the set should feel together

**Neighborhood streets:** shops and cafés below or beside houses and smaller apartments. Favor cream, coral, mint, and butter yellow. Let several adjacent buildings share a roof height, then break the run with a slightly taller corner block.

**Dense city streets:** more ordinary apartments, broad offices, and commercial blocks. Repeat related facades and window groups within the neighborhood scale.

**Workshop streets:** garages, low warehouses, utility buildings, and occasional small office blocks. Door sizes, loading openings, and roof profiles distinguish them. Keep the color palette welcoming.

**Transit and public streets:** stations, civic buildings, parking garages, and normal shops. Give public entrances stronger canopies or larger recesses so they read from the driving camera.

Buildings can repeat throughout the infinite city. The roster is a library of 150 distinct designs, not a rule that every block must contain a new one. Repetition makes neighborhoods recognizable.
